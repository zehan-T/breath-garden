## Ideation

### Ideas

I explored ten possible interactive projects related to singing, music, beauty, fashion, and performance. The strongest candidates were:

- **Breath Garden:** A breathing and voice-practice website where completing a guided exercise grows a unique digital flower. Its appeal is the combination of a useful practice routine with a beautiful, motivating visual reward. The main feasibility concerns are browser microphone access, reliable sound response, and avoiding medical or professional-training claims.
- **Warm-Up Roulette:** A tool that creates short vocal warm-up routines based on the User's available time and practice goal. It would be immediately useful to singers, but its central interaction is less visually distinctive, and all exercises would need careful sourcing and safety review.
- **Phrase Painter:** A creative experience that translates pitch, volume, and duration into flowing color and line. It offers a strong visual showcase, but it is more of an art generator than a tool that supports a repeatable practice habit.
- **Recital Lookbook:** A planning tool for coordinating concert clothing, shoes, jewelry, and changes between repertoire groups. It connects opera and fashion well, but image management and wardrobe planning are less closely connected to the problem I encounter most often in teaching.

### Choice

I chose **Breath Garden** because it grows directly from my experience as both a voice teacher and a voice student. Breathing, humming, and vocalizing are essential in lessons and individual practice, but their repetition can feel boring to children and adults alike. Breath Garden can make that invisible work visible: each completed exercise produces a flower and contributes to a garden that reflects consistent practice.

I set aside Warm-Up Roulette because it felt more like a conventional exercise library, Phrase Painter because it did not provide enough practical structure, and Recital Lookbook because it addressed a less important need in my own teaching. Some of their ideas may inform optional features, but they are not part of the approved core scope.

### Branding

The working title is **Breath Garden / 呼吸花园**. The logo shows thin, flowing breath lines transforming into a stem and an open flower. The current palette uses pale blue and soft lavender, with a bright background and gentle glow. The visual direction is dreamy, spacious, fresh, and elegant rather than childish or clinical. Typography will feel refined and airy, with generous spacing and clear readable interface text.

## Pitch

### Concept

Breath Garden is an interactive website that turns guided breathing and voice practice into a growing digital garden. A User chooses a mode and duration, follows a visual guide, and watches a flower develop in response to completing the exercise or making sound. At the end of the session, the User receives a unique flower that can be saved in a personal garden.

The first version will include **Breathe**, which works without a microphone, and **Hum**, which uses live microphone input. A later **Sing** mode may respond to a wider range of vocal sound if the core experience proves stable. The project is a motivational creative tool, not a medical device, vocal diagnosis system, or diving and breath-hold trainer.

### Title and branding

The title **Breath Garden / 呼吸花园** describes both parts of the experience: breath or voice is the input, and visible growth is the reward. The logo's moving line becomes a flower, expressing that transformation in one gesture. Pale blue and light lavender create a calm, dreamlike atmosphere. Soft light, organic movement, elegant typography, and an uncluttered bright layout should make the site feel beautiful, fresh, and relaxing.

### Why this project

I am both a voice student and a voice teacher. Breathing, humming, and vocal exercises are essential in my own practice and in every lesson I teach, yet the repetition can make them feel boring or difficult to sustain. This is especially noticeable when teaching children, but adult students also lose motivation.

Breath Garden will turn an abstract, repetitive exercise into visible growth. Completing a session creates a flower, while returning over time builds a larger garden. I want this sense of collection and progress to encourage regular practice without turning breathing or singing into a competitive score. The project also gives me a meaningful way to combine my knowledge of voice teaching with my interests in beauty, fashion, and polished visual design.

### Users

The primary Users are voice students, singers, and voice teachers who want a more engaging way to begin a lesson or practice session. Children may use it with a teacher, parent, or guardian. Secondary Users include actors, public speakers, wind players, yoga or mindfulness practitioners, and anyone who enjoys a short guided breathing break or sound-reactive art experience.

The project will not target divers or provide breath-hold training. It will not claim to increase lung capacity, treat anxiety, or diagnose vocal or respiratory conditions.

### Experience goals

Users should notice the calm, elegant visual world immediately and understand how to begin without separate instructions. They should be able to choose Breathe or Hum, choose a duration, complete a guided session, see an immediate visual response, and save the finished flower. The flower should feel like a reward and a record of participation, not a judgment of vocal quality.

The Breathe animation will expand and settle with the breathing guide. In Hum mode, sound duration will drive growth, relative loudness will influence scale or glow, and pitch movement may influence color. Every completed result should remain beautiful. If microphone permission is denied, Breathe mode must still work.

### Technical goals

The most important technical goal is a responsive loop from User action to visible growth. Browser microphone input must affect the flower quickly enough to feel connected to the User's voice. The system must also save completed flowers locally, restore them after a refresh, work on both phones and computers, and explain microphone privacy clearly.

Audio will be analyzed only in the browser. The project will not record or upload audio, require an account, or collect personal information. This keeps the first version private and technically manageable.

### Starting point

This project will be built from scratch using the Project 5 template. No previous project or open-source application will be used as its starting code. Any external libraries, reference material, fonts, sounds, or visual assets added later will be documented with their sources, licenses, and required credit in `research.md`.

### Tech stack

The project will use HTML for structure, CSS for responsive layout and animation, and JavaScript for the interaction flow and flower-generation rules. The Web Audio API will analyze live microphone input, SVG will support scalable flower graphics, and localStorage will preserve the User's garden on the device. GitHub Pages will provide public hosting.

My current experience is mainly with GitHub Pages, so live audio analysis and generative SVG graphics are new technical challenges. Avoiding a framework, server, database, and login system will keep the architecture understandable and appropriate for five weeks.

### MVP or critical function

The first critical proof is one flower that visibly grows in response to live microphone input. Once that works, the MVP will support one complete Breathe session and one complete Hum session: choose a mode and duration, follow the guide, grow a flower, view the result, save it locally, and find it again after refreshing the page.

The MVP succeeds when a new User can complete this loop without explanation; Hum responds clearly and promptly to sound; Breathe remains usable without microphone permission; saved flowers persist; and the experience works on a phone and a computer.

### Five-week map

- **Phase 1 — Ideate and pitch:** Define the concept, Users, scope, title, logo, visual branding, technical direction, and Pitch; obtain Studio director approval.
- **Phase 2 — Prove the core:** Test microphone permission and Web Audio input, then make one flower respond to sound in a deployed browser prototype.
- **Phase 3 — Build the experience:** Complete Breathe and Hum, duration selection, visual guidance, the result screen, and the first version of the personal garden.
- **Phase 4 — Refine and test:** Add Sing if the core is stable, unify the visual system, improve mobile behavior and accessibility, conduct User testing, and prepare a peer-review version.
- **Phase 5 — Finish and showcase:** Respond to peer feedback, simplify and stabilize the system, verify privacy and acceptance checks, publish the final version, and complete the breakdown and debrief.

### Stretch goals

In priority order:

1. Optional ambient music and sound effects with a clear mute control.
2. Practice streaks that unlock additional flower types.
3. Custom flower color themes.
4. Free arrangement of flowers within the personal garden.
5. Exporting a flower as a phone or computer wallpaper.
6. Sharing an individual flower or personal garden.

Lower items will be cut first if the core experience needs more time.

### Risks

- **Microphone reliability:** Permissions, background noise, and device differences may make audio response inconsistent. I will test the smallest sound-reactive prototype in Phase 2 on multiple devices before expanding it.
- **Scope growth:** Three modes, generative visuals, streaks, sharing, and sound design could exceed five weeks. Breathe and Hum define the MVP; Sing and all collection enhancements can be delayed or cut.
- **Health and safety interpretation:** Users could mistake the project for medical, vocal-diagnostic, or diving guidance. The interface will avoid health claims, breath-hold challenges, and performance scoring, and will include a concise safety boundary.
- **Privacy:** Microphone access may worry Users. Audio will be processed locally without recording or uploading, and the permission screen will explain this before access is requested.
- **Visual complexity:** Generative flowers could consume development time or become inconsistent. The first version will use a small set of reusable SVG rules so that every result remains coherent and attractive.
- **Limited technical experience:** Web Audio and procedural SVG are new to me. Phase 2 will validate them early, and the project will stay framework-free and server-free unless evidence shows a change is necessary.
